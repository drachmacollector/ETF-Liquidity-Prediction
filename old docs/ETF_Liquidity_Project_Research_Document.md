## 1. Executive Summary

**Thesis.** Indian ETFs rely on a primary-market arbitrage loop (market makers and large investors creating/redeeming units directly with the AMC) to keep exchange price near NAV. That loop breaks under identifiable conditions: physical supply shortages, demand surges, stale/mismatched NAV timing, regulatory capacity caps (overseas investment limits), and thin liquidity. Between October 2025 and September 2026 the Indian ETF market lived through four distinct stress episodes, and the regulator rewrote the price-band framework in response. No public, predictive, backtested early-warning system for this problem exists for India. The project builds one, and aims it at the people who actually have to manage the consequences: an ETF issuer's sales/distribution and product teams.

**What changed in the market during 2025-26 (and why the project scope is what it is):**

1. **Oct 2025: silver "supply-squeeze" premiums.** Silver ETFs traded 5-10% above iNAV for five straight sessions from 9 Oct 2025; at least eight AMCs suspended inflows into silver ETF fund-of-funds. [R]
2. **Late Jan-Feb 2026: precious-metals crash.** Silver fell roughly 30% in a single day (BIS) [V-source: BIS]; silver ETFs hit the 20% lower circuit on a 10-15% decline because the band was anchored to a stale T-2 NAV; exchanges improvised a T-1 reference on a Sunday special session. [R]
3. **15 Jun 2026: SEBI's new ETF framework** (T-1 last-30-minute VWAP base price, dynamic bands, pre-open call auction for gold/silver ETFs), **effective 7 Sep 2026** after a one-week extension (not 1 Sep). [V]
4. **Sep 2026: structural premiums on international ETFs.** Mirae's own NYSE FANG+ ETF traded at ~21-33% premium to NAV/iNAV because the industry overseas-investment cap keeps the primary market closed. [R]

**Key project decisions that follow from this research [REC]:**

- Keep the core objective: _forecast ETF price-vs-NAV dislocation and regulatory band stress before it happens, with probabilities, point-in-time honesty and a mandatory baseline._
- **Broaden the target from "premium to NAV" to a coupled set of risk signals:** (A) premium/discount distribution, (B) tail-event probability, (C) extreme-excursion/band-stress risk under the new rules, (D) structural (cap-driven) premium monitoring, (E) anomaly flag.
- **Use two historical validation events, not one** (Oct 2025 silver premium; Jan-Feb 2026 crash/circuit lock-ups), plus a **forward, immutable prediction ledger** starting November 2026 for the new regime.
- **Add a GenAI decision-support layer** (LLM-generated, number-grounded sales/product briefs and structured extraction of exchange/AMC notices) so the AI is central to usefulness for a sales organisation, not a bolt-on.
- **Include Mirae's international ETFs** in the universe: they are Mirae's most visible live premium problem and the same data pipeline serves them.

## 2. The Indian ETF Ecosystem and Market Mechanics

### 2.1 Structure

- An Indian ETF is an **open-ended mutual fund scheme whose units are listed on NSE and/or BSE**. It is governed by the SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 2026 (effective 1 Apr 2026) and the **SEBI Master Circular for Mutual Funds dated 20 Mar 2026** (Circular No. HO/24/13/11(1)2026-IMD-POD-1/I/7602/2026, in force from 1 Apr 2026, replacing the Master Circular of 27 Jun 2024). [V/R] Older scheme documents still cite clause numbers of the 2024 master circular; clause references must be re-mapped to the 2026 circular before being quoted. [A]
- The exchange-listed unit price is set by trading. The **NAV** is computed once per business day by the AMC and published through AMFI. The **iNAV** (indicative NAV) is an intraday estimate disseminated on exchanges and AMC sites.
- ETF categories relevant here: **equity** (broad, factor/smart-beta, sectoral, thematic), **debt** (incl. liquid/overnight), **commodity (gold, silver)**, **hybrid** (new: Mirae's 50:50 equity/G-Sec ETF, Jul 2026 [V]), and **international** (units backed by overseas ETFs/securities).

### 2.2 Creation/redemption (the arbitrage loop)

- **Market makers (MMs):** exchange members appointed by the AMC to provide two-way quotes. Scheme documents state an AMC appoints **at least two** market makers (HDFC FTSE India Equity ETF SID, 21 Sep 2026). [V] The older term "Authorised Participant" has been replaced by "Market Maker" in scheme documents following SEBI's 2022 liquidity framework. [R]
- **Direct creation/redemption:** MMs and eligible "large investors" transact directly with the AMC in multiples of a **creation unit** at intra-day NAV, against a portfolio deposit plus cash component. The NAV cut-off-time rule does not apply to MMs' direct transactions. [V] Examples: ICICI Prudential Silver ETF creation unit 30,000 units with a ₹25 crore minimum for non-MM direct applicants [V]; Mirae Asset BSE India Defence ETF basket 65,000 units [V]; Mirae Asset Silver ETF SID states large investors may deal directly above ₹25 crore. [V]
- **Why arbitrage can fail:** (i) the deposit basket cannot be sourced (physical silver/gold during a squeeze), (ii) the AMC suspends creation (e.g., overseas-investment cap), (iii) the NAV the AMC will pay is stale relative to the market that the ETF price reflects, (iv) MMs withdraw or widen quotes in thin or volatile conditions, (v) price bands or halts block the price from converging.
- **No public creation/redemption order-flow data exists** in India (unlike US share-outstanding/AP disclosures). Any "AP activity" feature is therefore a proxy. [A, supported by absence in all sources reviewed]

### 2.3 NAV, iNAV and market price: three prices, three clocks

| Price        | Producer         | Frequency                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Notes                                                                                                                                             |
| ------------ | ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Market price | Exchange trading | Continuous 09:15-15:30 IST (+ pre-open 09:00-09:15)                                                                                                                                                                                         | NSE/BSE close; see Section 6 for the closing/base price definition                                                                                |
| Official NAV | AMC via AMFI     | Once per business day, published after market hours                                                                                                                                                                                         | Authoritative, deep history; valuation timing per scheme (OQ-04)                                                                                  |
| iNAV         | AMC/exchange     | Scheme documents: "continuous basis… maximum time lag of 15 seconds" for equity ETFs, "or as per any other prescribed time" [V]. Commodity/debt/international disclosure frequency is lower and scheme-specific [C]; verify per SID (OQ-05) | **No free historical archive found** [R]; live views on NSE/BSE and AMC sites; commercial feeds exist (TrueData; BSE iNAV via Deutsche Börse) [R] |

**Measurement caveat (important):** a "premium" is only as good as the NAV/iNAV clock. When the underlying trades outside Indian hours (gold/silver globally, US/HK equities), the ETF price reacts instantly but the NAV/iNAV is stale, so some measured premium is information timing, not mispricing. Academic work on US ETFs makes the same point for illiquid or international holdings (Petajisto 2017). [R] The project therefore models premium _and_ the staleness structure explicitly (see Technical Specification).

| **A. | **D. ### 2.5 Flow context (why distributors care)

- Gold ETF inflows reached ~₹24,000 crore in Jan 2026; gold+silver ETF inflows ~₹33,500 crore; gold ETFs overtook equity funds on monthly flows for the first time. [R]
- Mirae's ETF product head stated gold/silver ETFs were **54% of all ETF flows in February 2026**. [R]
- ~3 million accounts were reported added at the peak just before prices reversed ("late-cycle rush"). [R]
- June 2026: gold ETFs -8.9% and silver ETFs -14.4% on average, yet flows turned positive again (~₹8,000 crore). [R]
  **Implication:** commodity ETFs are now a mass-retail, distributor-facing product, so mis-timed recommendations at a premium or into a crash carry direct client-outcome and reputational cost for the sales channel.

**What the evidence says about causality (and what remains inference):**

- The Oct 2025 event was a **primary-market capacity** failure (physical silver availability) and is the cleanest "premium" case. A pure price-vs-NAV model should be able to see building pressure.
- The Feb 2026 event is a **band-mechanics** case: the stale ±20%/T-2 NAV band pinned prices while the underlying kept moving. SEBI's own consultation framed the issue as the one-day base-price lag and bands not "commensurate with the price range of the underlying". [V]
- The inference that a stale base _contributed_ to lock-ups, and that the new base price would have reduced them, is the author's reading of the SEBI rationale. It is testable with a counterfactual replay (Technical Specification, band engine). [A]
- Whether the premium/discount in either event was _forecastable ahead of time_ from public daily data is the empirical question the project exists to answer. A negative result must be reported as such.

## 5. Why This Project, and What Was Screened Out

### 5.1 Selection criteria

(1) Genuine technical depth with AI at the centre; (2) data feasibility using India-accessible public data; (3) a live, dated, named real-world event for validation; (4) direct usefulness to an ETF sales/product team; (5) honesty of evaluation (baselines, point-in-time data, forward test); (6) novelty for the Indian market.

### 5.2 Ideas considered and rejected

| Idea                                                                                                                  | Reason rejected                                                                                                                                 |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Next-day ETF direction prediction (LSTM/RF on OHLC)                                                                   | Saturated, weak after costs, no distribution use                                                                                                |
| Generic tracking-error regression                                                                                     | Largely explained by simple factors (expense, replication); little room for AI                                                                  |
| HMM regime detection / sector rotation                                                                                | Commoditised indicator; not an ETF-distribution problem                                                                                         |
| RAG chatbot over factsheets                                                                                           | AI is not why it works; excluded as a standalone idea                                                                                           |
| Portfolio optimiser with ML forecasts                                                                                 | Portfolio-management problem, not sales/distribution                                                                                            |
| **Graph-based distributor lead scoring** (GNN link-prediction à la a Fidelity ICAIF 2022 paper, arXiv:2212.09624 [C]) | Most literal sales tool, but India has no 13F-style holder disclosure; only coarse channel-level data. Kept as a **future extension**, not core |
| Alt-data nowcasting of retail flows (Google Trends + news; Chen et al. 2023 [C])                                      | Valuable as a _feature module_; too thin standalone. Retained as an optional Phase 3 module with an ablation test                               |

### 5.3 Scope refinements made as a result of fresh research

1. **Add band-stress targets** because SEBI replaced the price-band mechanism (Section 6).
2. **Add a second validation event** (Jan-Feb 2026), which is a band-mechanics event and strengthens the case study.
3. **Treat the excursion label as regime-independent** (price excursion from the prior close is observable in history), which makes band-stress risk _partly backtestable now_ rather than purely forward-looking; caveats on censoring and behavioural feedback are specified in the Technical Specification. [REC]
4. **Add international-ETF structural-premium monitoring** (archetype D) as it is the largest, most persistent dislocation in Mirae's own product set.
5. **Add GenAI briefing and notice-extraction layers**, because the audience is a sales organisation.

### 6.4 Exchange implementation facts

- NSE conducted mock sessions on 29 Aug and 5 Sep 2026 for the revised pre-open framework and ETF norms; NEATPlus 7.9.0 mandatory from 5 Sep 2026. [V]
- NSE changed its **equity pre-open order-entry structure from 7 Sep 2026** (aligned with the Closing Auction Session framework). Secondary sources disagree on the exact timeline (one: 09:00-09:05 market+limit, 09:05-09:10 limit only, matching to 09:12, buffer to 09:15; another: 09:00-09:08 with random close 09:08-09:10). **Treat as unresolved until read from NSE/CMTR/74969 (OQ-02).** [R]
- The new regime was described by one broker Q&A as **not applicable to international ETFs** (e.g., MON100), while another source says all ETFs follow the T-1 VWAP base. The circular's text refers to "Equity, Debt and Commodity" ETFs and does not itself settle how overseas-underlying equity ETFs are classed. **Unresolved (OQ-01).** [R]
- BSE moved gold/silver ETF base to T-1 NAV by circular on Sunday 1 Feb 2026 as an interim measure. SEBI's consultation acknowledged exchanges adopted T-1 closing NAV/price as an interim step, feasible because of an intervening holiday. [R]
- As of 4 Nov 2026 **no public empirical evaluation** of the new regime (flex frequency, auction quality, premium behaviour) was found. [A: absence of evidence in searches]

### 6.5 Creation/redemption, market-making and iNAV rules (practical form)

- ≥2 market makers per ETF; AMC may change MMs; MMs provide two-way quotes; direct creation/redemption with the AMC in creation-unit multiples at intra-day NAV; large-investor threshold (₹25 crore in the SIDs reviewed); no cut-off timing for MM/large-investor direct dealings. [V via SIDs]
- iNAV disclosed on the exchange "on a continuous basis… maximum time lag of 15 seconds" for equity ETFs. [V via SID] Commodity-ETF frequency to be confirmed per scheme (OQ-05).
- AMFI daily performance/NAV disclosure obligation (Master Circular clause on performance disclosure) means every ETF's NAV is published for every business day. [V via SID citing the Master Circular]
- Trading-hours conventions: continuous session 09:15-15:30 IST; ETFs trade as equities in the equity (cash) segment, series EQ-type. [A/V partial]

### 6.6 International ETFs and the overseas-investment ceiling

- SEBI caps mutual-fund overseas investment at **US$7 billion industry-wide**, with a per-AMC limit (**US$1 billion**) and a separate **US$1 billion industry limit for overseas ETFs with a per-fund sub-limit (US$300 million)** per the SEBI circular of Jun 2021 and AMC SIDs (Mirae NYSE FANG+ SID). [V/R] Caps are measured against exposure on specific reference dates; once an AMC is at its limit it suspends subscriptions, and units can only be created when others redeem. [R]
- Consequence: international ETFs on the exchange become the "door that never shuts" and can trade 20-30%+ above NAV for long periods (Value Research, Sep 2026). [R]
- Regulatory watch: SEBI/RBI decisions on the cap are the single biggest driver of archetype D. Data on AMC-wise headroom and its publication frequency is an open item (OQ-08).

### 7.2 Known data-quality traps (design for each)

1. **Identifier drift:** NSE symbol changes (`SILVRETF`→`SILVERAG`), BSE scrip codes, AMFI scheme codes with multiple plan/option ISINs. Key on ISIN + AMFI scheme code; maintain symbol history.
2. **Two bhavcopy schemas** around 8 Jul 2024; normalise into one internal schema.
3. **NAV timing mismatch:** AMFI NAV date vs close; NAV published after the market closes; valuation time of commodity NAVs may differ from the ETF's closing VWAP window (OQ-04). A mismatched clock creates systematic apparent premium on volatile days.
4. **Special/Sunday sessions and holidays:** T-1 may be several calendar days earlier; special sessions exist (1 Feb 2026).
5. **Corporate actions on units** (splits/bonus/consolidation): adjust prices and NAV consistently; verify per ETF (OQ-19).
6. **Thin-trade days:** close price may be a stale LTP; use trades count/volume flags.
7. **Survivorship:** include merged/closed/renamed schemes.
8. **Point-in-time:** store `known_at` for each datum; AMFI NAVs can be revised.
9. **Scraping/ToS/rate-limits:** cache raw files immutably; polite crawling; prefer official archive hosts.

### 7.3 What is _not_ available (confirmed absence in this research)

Creation/redemption order flow or daily units outstanding; free historical iNAV; distributor/holder-level ownership; free tick-level history; public post-reform evaluation data.
